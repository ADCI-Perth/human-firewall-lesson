This is a companion lesson for students registered in the [ADCI microcredential][microcredential] on human factors in cyber security and privacy. **It is not free.** Access is part of the paid microcredential and is provided only to registered students. It is not an official Carpentries lesson. It uses the Carpentries Workbench format to present the material.

You do not need any technical background. Across twelve short episodes you will look at the people side of security: how attackers persuade, how identity and trust work, why insiders matter, how privacy law protects personal information, and what good security awareness looks like at work.

## Who this lesson is for

- Registered students in the microcredential who want extra practice.
- Students who work or volunteer in small organisations and handle other people's information.
- Students who manage teams and need to make sensible, low-cost security and privacy decisions.
- ADCI staff who teach the microcredential.

## How it relates to the microcredential

The microcredential is the assessed course run by ADCI. This lesson covers the same themes in a more practical, self-contained way, with its own activities and examples. Completing this lesson does not replace the microcredential's assessed work. Each episode ends with a short note on which week of the microcredential it matches.

## How to use it

**On your own.** Work through the episodes in order. Each takes about 45 to 60 minutes, including the activities. Try each challenge before you open the solution.

**With a group.** Each episode has instructor notes, which appear inline in Instructor View, and there is an instructor notes page with general advice. It is built for a single session, in person or online. Use the discussion prompts to bring in your own workplace examples.

Objectives are tagged ULO 1, 2 or 3, the three unit learning outcomes of SEC101 and the microcredential: ULO 1 is to analyse and respond to complex impacts on humans at the individual, group and organisational level, ULO 2 is to provide solutions to information and cyber issues in a personal or organisational setting and communicate the findings, and ULO 3 is to analyse privacy issues when working with digital systems and provide ethical and practical solutions.

Every episode follows the same pattern: questions and objectives, short teaching sections, two activities, a quick check, ideas to go deeper, a pointer to the credential, and a reference list.

## The example organisation

Many activities use Riverbend Community Library, a fictional small organisation. It is described on the learner pages so that every activity starts from the same facts.
