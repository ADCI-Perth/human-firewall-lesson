These notes help someone teaching this lesson live, in a workshop, class or study group. Each episode is designed to take 45 to 60 minutes of teaching and exercises. You do not need to be a security specialist, but read the episode and its worked solutions before you teach it.

## Running an episode live

### Timing

- Plan 45 to 60 minutes for each episode, using the minutes shown on the episode page.
- Each episode has about 20 minutes of teaching and about 35 minutes of challenges, quick check and discussion. Check the minutes on the episode page.
- If you run short, cut the last challenge before you cut the discussion. The discussion is where learners connect the ideas to their own workplaces.

### Sticky notes

- Give every learner a pad of sticky notes, or use a shared online board.
- Use two colours. Green means "I follow this" and pink means "I am stuck". Learners put the right colour on their laptop or the board at each break.
- For challenges, learners can write one idea per note, then group the notes on a wall or board.
- Read the pink notes at each break and answer the most common one first.

### Think-pair-share for discussions

1. **Think** (1 minute). Each learner writes their own answer to the discussion question, quietly.
2. **Pair** (2 minutes). Learners compare answers with a neighbour and agree on one point to share.
3. **Share** (2 minutes). Pairs report back. Write the points where everyone can see them, and ask what is missing.

Do not rush to give your own view. Ask a pair that disagrees to explain why.

### Using the Riverbend scenario

- Ask learners to read the Riverbend page before the episode that uses it, or allow 3 minutes at the start.
- Remind learners that the page does not list problems. Finding them is the exercise.
- If learners add facts that are not on the page, ask them to say they are assumptions.

### Challenges and solutions

- Learners should attempt each challenge before opening the solution.
- Solutions are worked answers. Learners' answers may differ and still be sound. Check the reasoning, not the exact wording.
