## About this scenario

Riverbend Community Library is a made-up organisation. This lesson uses it in several challenges so that you can practise on one consistent, realistic place. Any resemblance to a real library is a coincidence.

Read this page once. Come back to it whenever a challenge mentions Riverbend. It describes how the library works. It does not tell you what is right or wrong with it. Working that out is your job in the challenges.

## The organisation

Riverbend is a public library in a regional town. A local council runs it. It has 9 paid staff and about 25 volunteers. Volunteers shelve books, help at the front desk, run the weekend children's sessions and look after the café roster. The library is open six days a week, and some volunteers work alone on weekends.

## The information it holds

- **Member database.** It holds each member's name, address, date of birth, phone number, email address, borrowing history and concession status.
- **Children's program sign-up.** For each child, the library records the child's name, age, carer's contact details, allergies and whether the carer has consented to photos.
- **Volunteer roster.** It lists each volunteer, their shifts and their Working With Children Check number.
- **Café payments.** The café takes card payments (EFTPOS) through a third-party provider.
- **Public computers and guest Wi-Fi.** Members and visitors use a bank of public PCs and the library's guest Wi-Fi.

## How it works day to day

- The circulation desk PC is where books are borrowed and returned. Staff and volunteers on the desk use one shared staff login.
- Volunteers use their own phones. The roster is organised through a group chat, where shifts are swapped and changes are announced.
- The library sends a monthly newsletter about events and new books. The mailing list is run through a free online tool.
- The council supplies the network and some of the equipment. Day-to-day decisions about how the library runs are made by the library manager and the senior staff.

## What you will do with it

In the challenges you will look at Riverbend from different angles: the people who work and volunteer there, the information it holds, and the way that information moves around. Use only the facts on this page. If a challenge needs a detail that is not here, say what you are assuming.
